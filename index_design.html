Warmup LibraryEVERY TRAINING DAY POINTS TO ONE OF THESE THREE
Each training day in the Training tab shows which warmup and cooldown it uses. Look them up once here, apply them every time. Built around your tight spots: calves, ankles and shins plus shoulders, lats and chest. Tools: foam roller, bands, lacrosse ball. Cooldowns run 10 to 15 minutes.
The rules the research supports:
  • Warm up in 3 steps: raise your heart rate, move the joints you will load, then ramp toward working weight or pace (RAMP model).
  • Short static holds before training are fine. Under 60 s per muscle costs about 1 to 2% strength or speed, and a dynamic warmup after it erases that. Long holds of 60 s or more before lifting or sprinting are the problem.
  • Foam rolling before training is safe. It adds short-term range of motion and does not hurt strength or speed. 30 to 60 s per area is enough.
  • Flexibility comes from weekly volume. Aim for about 5 min of stretching per tight muscle per week. 2 holds of 30 to 45 s, 4 to 5 days a week, gets you there. Cooldown is the easiest time to bank it.
  • A cooldown does not stop soreness. The easy jog or walk brings your heart rate down. The stretching and rolling are for range of motion, not recovery speed.
  • No cold water after lifting. Cold immersion right after strength work reduced muscle growth in a 2024 meta-analysis. Cold is fine after runs and rucks.
  • Shins are a strength problem first. Runners with shin splints do fewer calf raises than healthy runners. Calf and tibialis work is in the run and ruck warmups for that reason.
